Fundamentals

What is medical technology?

Medical technology encompasses all devices, instruments, software and procedures used to diagnose, monitor, treat or alleviate diseases. Here we explain the fundamentals – clearly and in a structured way.

Definition

Medical technology is the application of engineering principles and technologies in medicine. It ranges from the simple clinical thermometer to ultrasound and X-ray equipment, ventilators, dialysis systems and large-scale imaging systems.

Legally, these products are referred to as medical devices. In the European Union, the Medical Device Regulation (MDR, Regulation (EU) 2017/745) defines the requirements a product must meet before it may bear the CE marking and be placed on the market.

Unlike a medicinal product, a medical device achieves its principal intended action not by pharmacological means but by physical means: for example through measurement, imaging, mechanical support or the delivery of energy.

Why medical technology is so important

Modern medicine would be unthinkable without medical technology. At every stage of care, it helps to treat patients safely, quickly and precisely.

Early and accurate diagnosis

Imaging and laboratory diagnostic procedures make diseases visible, often long before symptoms appear.

Effective therapy

From infusion pumps to dialysis: therapy devices support or replace vital bodily functions.

Safety and monitoring

Monitoring systems record vital signs continuously and raise an alarm before a condition becomes critical.

Hygiene and patient protection

Sterilisation and reprocessing prevent infections and protect patients and staff.

Efficiency in everyday care

Good equipment speeds up workflows, eases the burden on staff and lowers costs in the long run.

Risk classes of medical devices

The MDR divides medical devices into four classes according to their risk potential. The higher the class, the stricter the requirements for evidence, testing and surveillance. In-vitro diagnostic devices such as laboratory analysers are covered by a separate regulation, the IVDR (Regulation (EU) 2017/746), with classes A to D.

Low risk

Class I

Non-invasive or simple products with low hazard potential.

Examples: wheelchairs, dressings, simple examination couches

Medium risk

Class IIa

Products with moderate risk, often active devices for diagnosis or short-term use.

Examples: ultrasound scanners, hearing aids, many dental instruments

Elevated risk

Class IIb

Products with elevated risk, such as active devices that deliver energy or support vital functions.

Examples: X-ray equipment, ventilators, infusion pumps, dialysis machines

High risk

Class III

Products with the highest risk, in particular implants and products in contact with the heart, the central circulatory system or the central nervous system.

Examples: pacemakers, heart valves, joint implants

The life cycle of a medical device

Buying a device is only the beginning. Those who keep the entire service life in mind make better decisions.

  1. Needs & planning

    Clarification of clinical needs, rooms, interfaces and budget.

  2. Procurement

    Requirements profile, quotation comparison, negotiation, ordering and acceptance.

  3. Commissioning & training

    Installation, functional testing and documented training of staff.

  4. Operation & maintenance

    Maintenance, safety and metrological inspections, documentation in the medical device logbook.

  5. Decommissioning

    Proper shutdown, data erasure and disposal or resale.

Key terms

MDR
Medical Device Regulation – EU Regulation 2017/745 on medical devices. It defines the requirements products must meet before being placed on the market.
CE marking
Confirms that a medical device meets the applicable EU requirements. For higher risk classes, a Notified Body is involved.
MPDG & MPBetreibV
The German Medical Devices Law Implementation Act (MPDG) and the German Medical Devices Operator Ordinance (MPBetreibV) regulate, among other things, the obligations when operating and using medical devices.
STK / MTK
Safety-related and metrological inspections (German: sicherheitstechnische bzw. messtechnische Kontrollen) – regular checks that are mandatory for certain devices.
Medical device logbook (Medizinproduktebuch)
Documentation for certain active medical devices: training, functional tests, maintenance and malfunctions.
UDI
Unique Device Identification – a unique product identifier for the traceability of medical devices.
