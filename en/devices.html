Medical imaging 4 devices

Imaging devices form the basis for diagnosis, treatment planning and follow-up without opening the body. The methods differ greatly in operating principle, radiation exposure, space and construction requirements and running costs. This section explains the most important device types in plain language and shows what matters in selection and procurement.

Marked as learned

Ultrasound system (sonography)

Imaging with sound waves – no radiation

Ultrasound systems produce real-time images of organs, vessels and soft tissue using high-frequency sound waves – radiation-free and directly at the bedside.

View device
Marked as learned

Digital X-ray system

Projection radiography with a digital detector

Digital X-ray systems use X-rays to produce projection images of bones, lungs and other structures; a flat-panel detector delivers the image on screen within seconds.

View device
Marked as learned

Computed tomography scanner (CT)

Cross-sectional images from rotating X-rays

A CT scanner uses many X-ray projections to create overlap-free cross-sectional images and 3D reconstructions of the body – fast and detailed, for example in stroke, trauma or cancer diagnostics.

View device
Marked as learned

Magnetic resonance imaging scanner (MRI)

Cross-sectional imaging with a magnetic field and radio waves

The MRI scanner images soft tissue, brain, joints and organs without X-rays – using a strong magnetic field, radio waves and excellent soft-tissue contrast.

View device

Laboratory diagnostics 4 devices

Laboratory diagnostics covers devices that automatically analyse body samples such as blood, serum or urine. The results support physicians in diagnosis, therapy monitoring and prevention. Typical systems range from compact practice-based instruments to fully automated high-throughput analysers.

Marked as learned

Haematology analyser

Automated counting and differentiation of blood cells

A haematology analyser counts and classifies the cells in the blood and produces a full blood count, with or without differential, within minutes. It is part of the basic equipment of almost every medical laboratory.

View device
Marked as learned

Clinical chemistry analyser

Metabolic values from serum, plasma and urine

A clinical chemistry analyser measures the concentration of substances such as glucose, electrolytes, enzymes and cholesterol in serum, plasma or urine. It provides the basis for assessing organ function and metabolism.

View device
Marked as learned

Blood gas analyser

pH, oxygen and carbon dioxide within minutes

A blood gas analyser measures pH, oxygen and carbon dioxide partial pressures as well as electrolytes and metabolites in blood within a few minutes. It is indispensable for monitoring critically ill and ventilated patients.

View device
Marked as learned

Laboratory centrifuge

Separating samples by centrifugal force

A laboratory centrifuge separates blood and other liquids into their components, such as cells and plasma, by rapid rotation. It is an indispensable preparation device for almost every laboratory analysis.

View device

Patient monitoring 4 devices

Patient monitoring covers devices that continuously measure and display heart rate, blood pressure, oxygen saturation, respiration and other vital signs. They make critical changes visible before they become emergencies and provide the basis for decisions in intensive care, the operating theatre, the emergency department and increasingly in practices and nursing care. The range extends from multi-parameter monitoring to a simple finger sensor.

Marked as learned

Patient monitor

Multi-parameter monitoring of vital functions

A patient monitor records several vital signs at once, displays them as waveforms and numeric values and raises an alarm when limits are exceeded. It is the central monitoring device in intensive care units, operating theatres and emergency departments.

View device
Marked as learned

ECG machine (12-lead)

Recording the electrical activity of the heart

A 12-lead ECG machine records the electrical activity of the heart through ten electrodes and presents it as twelve leads. It is the standard procedure for diagnosing cardiac arrhythmias and impaired blood supply to the heart muscle.

View device
Marked as learned

Pulse oximeter

Oxygen saturation and pulse without drawing blood

A pulse oximeter measures blood oxygen saturation (SpO2) and pulse rate non-invasively via a sensor on the finger, ear or foot. It is one of the most widely used monitoring devices in hospitals, practices and ambulance services.

View device
Marked as learned

Fetal monitor (CTG)

Monitoring fetal heartbeat and contractions

A fetal monitor (cardiotocograph, CTG) simultaneously records the heart rate of the unborn child and the mother's uterine contractions. It is used in antenatal care and during labour to monitor the well-being of the baby.

View device

Surgical technology 4 devices

In the operating theatre, many devices work closely together: the anaesthesia machine keeps the patient safely anaesthetised, the electrosurgical unit cuts and stops bleeding, the endoscopy system enables minimally invasive procedures, and the surgical light provides optimal visibility. In procurement, room planning, gas supply, reprocessing and training are as decisive as the device technology itself. This section explains the key device types and shows what to look for in selection and procurement.

Marked as learned

Anaesthesia machine

Gas dosing, ventilation and monitoring under anaesthesia

Anaesthesia machines (anaesthesia workstations) dose oxygen, air and anaesthetic gases, ventilate the patient during surgery and continuously monitor the respiratory gases.

View device
Marked as learned

Electrosurgical unit (ESU)

Cutting and coagulating with high-frequency current

Electrosurgical units generate high-frequency alternating current that cuts tissue almost without bleeding and seals blood vessels by coagulation.

View device
Marked as learned

Endoscopy system (video endoscopy tower)

Camera, light and monitor for minimally invasive surgery

An endoscopy system consists of an endoscope, camera, light source, monitor and auxiliary devices; it enables diagnosis and surgery through natural body openings or small incisions.

View device
Marked as learned

Surgical light

Low-shadow, colour-accurate light for the surgical field

Surgical lights illuminate the surgical field brightly, with minimal shadows and accurate colours, without appreciably heating the tissue; they are mounted on the ceiling, wall or a stand and can be positioned freely.

View device

Therapy 4 devices

Therapy devices intervene directly in body functions: they ventilate, deliver medication, purify the blood or terminate dangerous cardiac arrhythmias. Because errors here can immediately endanger patients, the requirements for alarms, reliability and user training are high. In this category you will learn about the most important device groups in intensive care, emergency medicine and renal replacement therapy.

Marked as learned

Ventilator

Mechanical support or replacement of breathing

A ventilator takes over or supports breathing for patients who cannot breathe sufficiently on their own. It delivers a mixture of air and oxygen into the lungs with controlled pressure and volume.

View device
Marked as learned

Infusion pump / syringe pump

Precise delivery of drugs and fluids

Infusion pumps deliver fluids and medication into the body at a controlled, adjustable rate, usually via a venous access. Syringe pumps are designed for small volumes and highly potent drugs.

View device
Marked as learned

Defibrillator

Controlled shock against life-threatening arrhythmias

A defibrillator delivers a controlled electric shock to the heart to terminate life-threatening arrhythmias such as ventricular fibrillation. Depending on the design, the range extends from automated external defibrillators (AED) for lay rescuers to manual devices with a monitor for professionals.

View device
Marked as learned

Dialysis machine

Blood purification as renal replacement therapy

In kidney failure, a dialysis machine takes over the removal of toxins and excess water from the blood. The blood is routed outside the body through a filter in which waste products and surplus fluid are removed.

View device

Dental technology 4 devices

Dental technology covers all equipment used in dental practices, orthodontics, oral and maxillofacial surgery and dental laboratories. This includes treatment units, dental imaging, digital impressions and instrument reprocessing. Many of these devices are closely interconnected, which is why interfaces and hygiene requirements are particularly important in procurement.

Marked as learned

Dental treatment unit

The central workstation of the dental practice

The treatment unit combines the patient chair, instruments, water and air supply, suction and lighting in a single workstation. It is the heart of every dental treatment room.

View device
Marked as learned

Panoramic X-ray unit (OPG)

Overview image of both jaws in a single exposure

The panoramic X-ray unit produces an overview image of the upper and lower jaw, all teeth, the temporomandibular joints and the maxillary sinuses in a single image. It is the most important overview examination in dentistry.

View device
Marked as learned

Intraoral scanner

Digital impressions instead of impression trays

The intraoral scanner captures teeth and gums optically and generates a three-dimensional digital model from the data. It replaces the conventional impression with impression material.

View device
Marked as learned

Dental autoclave (class B)

Steam sterilisation for instruments and handpieces

The dental autoclave sterilises instruments, handpieces and contra-angles with saturated steam under pressure. Class B units can also reliably sterilise wrapped, hollow and porous loads.

View device

Sterilisation & hygiene 4 devices

The reprocessing of medical devices follows a fixed sequence: after pre-cleaning – with pre-treatment in an ultrasonic bath if required – instruments are cleaned and disinfected automatically in a washer-disinfector, then inspected, packaged, sealed and sterilized in a steam sterilizer. Every step must be validated and documented, because gaps in reprocessing endanger patients and breach legal requirements. This section explains the key reprocessing devices and shows what to look for in selection and procurement.

Marked as learned

Steam Sterilizer (Autoclave)

Sterilization with saturated steam under pressure

Steam sterilizers (autoclaves) use saturated, pressurized steam to destroy all microorganisms including spores on packaged instruments and are the standard sterilization method in hospitals and practices.

View device
Marked as learned

Washer-Disinfector (WD)

Automated cleaning and thermal disinfection

Washer-disinfectors clean and disinfect instruments automatically in a validated programme and thus replace error-prone manual reprocessing.

View device
Marked as learned

Ultrasonic Cleaner

Pre-cleaning by cavitation in a liquid bath

Ultrasonic cleaners use high-frequency sound waves in a cleaning bath to loosen even stubborn soil from joints, grooves and cavities of instruments and complement automated reprocessing.

View device
Marked as learned

Heat Sealer for Sterile Packaging

Sealing sterilization packaging as a microbial barrier

Heat sealers seal transparent paper-film pouches and reels to form a sterile barrier that is impermeable to microorganisms, so that instruments remain sterile from sterilization until use.
