Dialysis machine

Blood purification as renal replacement therapy

In kidney failure, a dialysis machine takes over the removal of toxins and excess water from the blood. The blood is routed outside the body through a filter in which waste products and surplus fluid are removed.

How it works

A blood pump draws blood via a vascular access, usually a shunt in the arm or a catheter, into an extracorporeal circuit. In the dialyser, a filter containing thousands of fine hollow fibres, the blood flows along a semi-permeable membrane. On the other side of the membrane, the dialysis fluid flows in the opposite direction.

Waste products such as urea and creatinine as well as excess electrolytes pass into the dialysis fluid by diffusion. Excess water is removed by a controlled pressure difference (ultrafiltration), with the amount precisely regulated by the machine. The machine prepares the dialysis fluid from purified water and concentrates at the correct composition and temperature.

Numerous sensors monitor the pressures in the blood circuit, air bubbles, blood leaks and the conductivity of the dialysis fluid. A treatment usually lasts several hours and is typically performed three times a week. In haemodiafiltration, part of the plasma water is additionally replaced with a substitution solution in order to remove larger molecules as well.

Areas of use

  • Dialysis centres and nephrology practices
  • Hospital dialysis units
  • Intensive care unit (acute kidney failure, continuous procedures)
  • Home dialysis
  • Certain cases of poisoning

What to look for when procuring

  1. Plan the water treatment as part of the project: dialysis machines require purified water according to the applicable standards (reverse osmosis, ring main) and drains at every treatment station.
  2. Clarify the required procedures (haemodialysis, online haemodiafiltration, continuous procedures for intensive care) and suitability for home dialysis.
  3. Consider the running costs of dialysers, tubing sets, concentrates and disinfectants; over the service life they often exceed the purchase price of the machine.
  4. Pay attention to interfaces with dialysis documentation software and to standardised operating concepts when several machines are run.
  5. Agree on a service contract, periodic safety inspections (STK) under the German Medical Devices Operator Ordinance (MPBetreibV) and microbiological monitoring of water quality, and provide structured training for nursing staff.

Key terms

Dialyser
The actual filter with a semi-permeable membrane in which substances are exchanged between blood and dialysis fluid.
Shunt
A surgically created connection between an artery and a vein in the arm that provides a permanent vascular access with high blood flow.
Ultrafiltration
Removal of water from the blood by a pressure difference across the membrane.
