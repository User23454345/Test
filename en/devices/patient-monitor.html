Patient monitor

Multi-parameter monitoring of vital functions

A patient monitor records several vital signs at once, displays them as waveforms and numeric values and raises an alarm when limits are exceeded. It is the central monitoring device in intensive care units, operating theatres and emergency departments.

Back to overview

How it works

Through electrodes, sensors and cuffs, the monitor picks up measurement signals directly from the patient. Typical parameters are ECG with heart rate, oxygen saturation (SpO2), non-invasive blood pressure (NIBP), respiratory rate and body temperature. Depending on the configuration, invasive blood pressure, capnography (etCO2) or cardiac output can be added.

The signals are processed digitally, displayed as waveforms and numeric values and checked against adjustable alarm limits. If a limit is exceeded or undershot, the monitor issues visual and audible alarms with graded priority. Trend views show the course over hours and make assessment easier.

Many monitors have a modular design: measurement modules can be plugged in or removed as needed. Via a network connection, the data can be transmitted to a central station and to the patient data management system so that staff can keep an eye on several beds at the same time.

Areas of use

  • Intensive care and intermediate care units
  • Operating theatre and recovery room
  • Emergency department and resuscitation room
  • Patient transport inside and outside the hospital
  • Monitoring of at-risk patients on general wards

What to look for when procuring

  1. Define the required parameter set per unit (basic: ECG, SpO2, NIBP, temperature; intensive care: additionally invasive pressure and capnography) and make sure it can be upgraded with modules later.
  2. Check the interfaces to the central station and the hospital information system (e.g. HL7) as well as compatibility with existing monitors and the infrastructure (network, Wi-Fi).
  3. Calculate the running costs for consumables such as electrodes, SpO2 sensors, blood pressure cuffs and temperature probes; proprietary sensors can have a considerable impact on ongoing costs.
  4. Consider alarm management and ergonomics: a consistent alarm philosophy across the facility, good readability, simple operation and surfaces that are easy to clean.
  5. Agree on a service contract, staff training and backup devices for downtime. Observe the inspection duties under the Medical Devices Operator Ordinance (in Germany: MPBetreibV): periodic safety inspections (STK) for monitors with invasive pressure measurement and metrological checks (MTK) for the non-invasive blood pressure function.

Key terms

SpO2
Oxygen saturation of haemoglobin in arterial blood measured by pulse oximetry, given as a percentage.
NIBP
Non-invasive blood pressure measurement with an upper-arm cuff that the monitor inflates automatically at adjustable intervals.
Capnography
Continuous measurement of the carbon dioxide content in exhaled air (etCO2) to monitor ventilation and circulation.
