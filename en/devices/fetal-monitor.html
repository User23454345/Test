Fetal monitor (CTG)

Monitoring fetal heartbeat and contractions

A fetal monitor (cardiotocograph, CTG) simultaneously records the heart rate of the unborn child and the mother's uterine contractions. It is used in antenatal care and during labour to monitor the well-being of the baby.

Back to overview

How it works

An ultrasound Doppler sensor on the mother's abdomen detects the movements of the fetal heart and calculates the fetal heart rate from them. A second sensor, a pressure transducer (tocodynamometer), registers the tension of the abdominal wall and thus the frequency and duration of contractions. Both sensors are held in place with elastic belts.

The device displays both signals as parallel traces over time, on paper or on screen. Assessment takes into account the baseline rate, the variability and the accelerations and decelerations of the fetal heart rate in relation to the contractions. This allows signs of an insufficient oxygen supply to the baby to be recognised.

Many devices additionally monitor the maternal pulse and oxygen saturation to avoid confusion with the fetal heart rate. For multiple pregnancies there are devices with two or three heart rate channels, and wireless sensors give the pregnant woman more freedom of movement.

Areas of use

  • Antenatal care in practices and hospitals
  • Delivery room during labour and induction of labour
  • Monitoring of high-risk and post-term pregnancies
  • Inpatient monitoring for preterm contractions
  • Multiple pregnancies

At a glance

Risk class (MDR)
IIa or IIb – depending on intended purpose (intrapartum monitoring usually IIb)
Typical settings
Gynaecology practice, delivery room, obstetric ward
Space requirement
Tabletop or cart device next to the examination couch or delivery bed
Typical service life
Around 8 to 10 years

What to look for when procuring

  1. Determine whether a singleton or a multiple-pregnancy CTG is required and whether maternal parameters (pulse, SpO2, blood pressure) should also be recorded.
  2. For the delivery room, central monitoring with connection of several devices and digital archiving of the recordings (documentation requirements) is an important criterion.
  3. Check consumables and accessories: recording paper, ultrasound gel, belts, and rechargeable wireless or waterproof sensors for the birthing pool.
  4. Pay attention to signal quality in obese patients and during movement, and to easy sensor positioning by staff.
  5. Arrange training in CTG interpretation, a service contract and checks as specified by the manufacturer; in Germany, safety inspections (STK) and metrological checks (MTK) under the MPBetreibV are not mandatory for CTG devices, but an integrated non-invasive blood pressure module is subject to a metrological check every two years.

Key terms

Cardiotocography (CTG)
Simultaneous recording of the fetal heart rate (cardio) and uterine contractions (toco).
Tocodynamometer
Pressure transducer on the abdominal wall that registers contractions without measuring their actual strength.
Variability
Natural beat-to-beat fluctuation of the fetal heart rate; an important sign of the baby's well-being.
