Dental treatment unit

The central workstation of the dental practice

The treatment unit combines the patient chair, instruments, water and air supply, suction and lighting in a single workstation. It is the heart of every dental treatment room.

Back to overview

How it works

A treatment unit consists of an electrically adjustable patient chair, a dentist's element with instrument holders, an assistant's element with suction, and a water unit with cuspidor and rinsing cup. A foot pedal lets the practitioner control instrument speed and functions without using their hands.

The instruments – turbine, micromotor with contra-angle handpiece, multifunction syringe and ultrasonic scaler – are supplied with compressed air, water and electricity through hoses. The air-driven turbine reaches very high rotational speeds, while the electric micromotor delivers higher torque at lower speed. A spray of water and air cools the tooth during drilling.

The suction system removes saliva, water and aerosols from the patient's mouth and passes them through an amalgam separator into the drain. Integrated water disinfection systems keep the water lines hygienic, as standing water would otherwise form biofilm. Modern units also feature an LED operating light and touch panels with stored chair positions.

Areas of use

  • General dentistry and prophylaxis
  • Filling and crown treatments
  • Endodontics (root canal treatment)
  • Orthodontics and paediatric dentistry
  • Oral surgery procedures in the practice

At a glance

Risk class (MDR)
usually IIa (unit with powered instruments); patient chair alone: I
Typical settings
Dental practices, dental clinics, orthodontics, oral and maxillofacial surgery
Space requirement
Treatment room with connections for water, compressed air, waste water and electricity; check the installation plan in advance
Typical service life
around 10–15 years with regular maintenance

What to look for when procuring

  1. Clarify the installation requirements before purchase: connections for water, compressed air, vacuum/suction, waste water and electricity must be located in the floor or wall at exactly the right position.
  2. Choose the instrument configuration according to your range of treatments: number of micromotors and turbines, ultrasonic scaler, curing light and, if required, an endodontic motor function.
  3. Look for hygiene features such as automatic flushing programmes, water line disinfection and removable, thermally disinfectable parts – they make compliance with hygiene requirements considerably easier.
  4. An amalgam separator is a legal requirement in the EU – including Germany – and in many other countries; check its approval, maintenance interval and disposal routes.
  5. Compare service contracts, technical response times and spare-parts availability – if the unit fails, the entire treatment room is out of action.

Key terms

Turbine
Air-driven handpiece with a very high rotational speed used to prepare hard tooth tissue.
Amalgam separator
Filter unit in the waste-water line that retains mercury-containing amalgam residues so they do not enter the sewer system.
Contra-angle handpiece
Attachment for the micromotor that transfers rotation to the bur; available with speed-increasing or speed-reducing gearing.
