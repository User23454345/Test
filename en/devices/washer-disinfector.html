Washer-Disinfector (WD)

Automated cleaning and thermal disinfection

Washer-disinfectors clean and disinfect instruments automatically in a validated programme and thus replace error-prone manual reprocessing.

Back to overview

How it works

The instruments are placed in the chamber in mesh baskets or on special loading carts, and hollow instruments are connected to flushing adapters so that they are flushed internally. The programme starts with a cold-water pre-rinse that loosens blood and tissue residues before heat could coagulate them.

In the cleaning phase, warm water with a usually alkaline or enzymatic detergent circulates through the chamber via spray arms. Intermediate rinses follow and, if necessary, neutralization with an acidic agent. For thermal disinfection, the demineralized rinse water is heated to 90 to 93 °C and held for a few minutes, which corresponds to a defined A0 value, usually 3000.

Finally, a hot-air blower dries the load so that it can be inspected and packaged immediately. All programme parameters such as temperature, time and dosing quantity are recorded and stored as a batch record. Validation according to EN ISO 15883 demonstrates that the device reproducibly achieves the required result with the loads and chemicals used.

Areas of use

  • CSSD in hospitals with several large units
  • Reprocessing room in surgical and dental practices
  • Endoscopy departments with special endoscope washer-disinfectors for flexible endoscopes (chemo-thermal disinfection at lower temperature)
  • Reprocessing of anaesthesia and ventilation accessories
  • Cleaning of sterilization containers and theatre shoes in large chamber units

At a glance

Risk class (MDR)
IIa; IIb if disinfection of invasive devices (e.g. flexible endoscopes) is the end point of processing (MDR Rule 16)
Typical settings
CSSD, reprocessing rooms in practices, endoscopy units
Space requirement
Under-counter or free-standing unit from approx. 60 cm wide; pass-through units as a barrier between the dirty and clean sides
Typical service life
approx. 10 to 12 years

What to look for when procuring

  1. Define capacity and loading systems: the number of mesh baskets per cycle and inserts for hollow instruments, dental handpieces, anaesthesia accessories or instruments for minimally invasive surgery determine whether the device fits the actual instrument inventory.
  2. Plan room and connections: cold, hot and demineralized water, a temperature-resistant drain, three-phase power and, for pass-through units, a structural separation into dirty and clean sides; take exhaust air and heat load in the room into account.
  3. Process chemicals and dosing: detergent, neutralizer and rinse aid must be matched to the device and the instrument materials; automatic dosing pumps with level monitoring prevent faulty cycles.
  4. Plan for validation according to EN ISO 15883 and regular performance qualification, including process challenge devices for cleaning performance and protein detection; without a current validation, the batch may not be released.
  5. Documentation, service and training: archive batch records digitally, check integration with instrument tracking systems, agree on a service contract with maintenance and spare parts, and provide staff with user training as required by the German Medical Devices Operator Ordinance (MPBetreibV).

Key terms

A0 value
Measure of the inactivation of microorganisms by moist heat; an A0 value of 3000 corresponds, for example, to 90 °C for five minutes and is the usual value for the thermal disinfection of instruments.
Thermal disinfection
Disinfection solely by hot water without chemical disinfectants; it is reliable, easy to validate and leaves no residues.
Flushing adapter
Connector that links hollow instruments such as cannulas or tubing to the water circuit so that internal surfaces are cleaned as well.
