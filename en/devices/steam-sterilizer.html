Steam steriliser (autoclave)

Sterilisation with saturated steam under pressure

Steam sterilisers (autoclaves) use saturated, pressurised steam to destroy all microorganisms including spores on packaged instruments and are the standard sterilisation method in hospitals and practices.

How it works

Air is first removed from the sterilisation chamber, in class B devices by a fractionated pre-vacuum with several evacuation and steam pulses. Only in this way can the steam also penetrate hollow items such as tubing and handpieces as well as porous textiles. Pure steam is then admitted until pressure and temperature reach the set point.

The actual sterilisation takes place at 134 °C and about 2 bar gauge pressure for at least three minutes, or at 121 °C for at least 15 minutes. The hot, moist steam condenses on the load, transfers its energy and denatures the proteins of the microorganisms. The combination of temperature, moisture, pressure and time is decisive, so the device monitors all parameters continuously.

After the holding time the steam is released and the load is dried in a post-vacuum so that the packaging stays dry and thus maintains its microbial barrier. Every cycle is recorded with its pressure, temperature and time profile, and batch monitoring, helix test and Bowie-Dick test additionally prove steam penetration. This release documentation is mandatory and must be retained.

Areas of use

  • Central sterile services department (CSSD) in hospitals
  • Reprocessing room in medical practices and outpatient clinics
  • Dental practices (benchtop autoclaves)
  • Laboratories and nursing facilities
  • Sterilisation of textiles, hollow items and packaged instrument sets

What to look for when procuring

  1. Match device class and chamber volume to the volume of goods to be sterilised: class B according to EN 13060 for packaged hollow items and textiles in practices, large sterilisers according to EN 285 for the CSSD; the chamber size should cover the daily volume with some capacity to spare.
  2. Check water and power supply: autoclaves need demineralised or distilled water (treatment unit or canisters), depending on the model a drain connection or a waste-water tank and, depending on size, three-phase power and adequate room ventilation because of the heat output.
  3. Documentation and interfaces: batch documentation via USB, network or printer, ideally linked to instrument tracking or practice management software, simplifies release and compliance with record-keeping obligations.
  4. Plan for validation: after installation, a performance qualification by a qualified service provider is required, followed by regular, usually annual, requalification; include these costs in the overall calculation.
  5. Budget for the service contract and consumables: door seals, filters, water treatment cartridges, indicators and process challenge devices are running costs; set maintenance intervals and response times in the contract and ensure staff receive documented user training.

Key terms

Fractionated pre-vacuum
Repeated evacuation of air alternating with steam admission before sterilisation; a prerequisite for steam to fully reach narrow cavities.
Bowie-Dick test
Daily test with an indicator pack that proves air removal and steam penetration in the chamber.
Batch (load)
All items of one sterilisation cycle; they are documented, checked and released together.
