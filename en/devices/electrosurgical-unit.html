Electrosurgical unit (ESU)

Cutting and coagulating with high-frequency current

Electrosurgical units generate high-frequency alternating current that cuts tissue almost without bleeding and seals blood vessels by coagulation.

Back to overview

How it works

The device generates alternating current at frequencies of several hundred kilohertz. At these frequencies nerves and muscles are not stimulated, but the current acts on tissue through heat. At the small tip of the active electrode the current density is very high, so the tissue there is heated in a targeted way.

During cutting, the cell water evaporates abruptly and the tissue separates; during coagulation it is heated more slowly, so that proteins clot and small vessels are sealed. The effect can be adjusted via power, waveform and modulation (cut, coagulation, blended modes). Modern units regulate the power automatically depending on the tissue resistance.

In the monopolar technique, the current flows from the instrument through the body to a large return electrode (neutral electrode) attached to the patient. In the bipolar technique, it flows only between the two jaws of forceps or a clamp, which confines the current path to the target tissue. Monitoring of the return electrode detects poor skin contact and prevents burns.

Areas of use

  • General and visceral surgery
  • Gynaecology, urology and ENT
  • Laparoscopic and endoscopic procedures
  • Dermatology and outpatient minor surgery
  • Vessel sealing in major resections

What to look for when procuring

  1. Define the range of procedures: is a basic monopolar/bipolar unit sufficient, or are additional functions such as vessel sealing, argon plasma coagulation or special modes for endoscopy required?
  2. Check compatibility of the instruments: connector systems for handpieces, bipolar forceps and return electrodes are not consistently standardised across manufacturers; budget for adapters or new instruments.
  3. Calculate consumable costs: single-use return electrodes, electrode tips and single-use instruments for vessel sealing make up a large share of the running costs.
  4. Require safety functions: return electrode monitoring (contact quality), automatic power regulation and a surgical smoke evacuation system to protect the operating theatre staff.
  5. Observe training and inspection duties: electrosurgical units are listed in Annex 1 of the German Medical Devices Operator Ordinance (MPBetreibV) and are subject to periodic safety inspections (STK), a mandatory medical device logbook and documented user training; users must be trained in risks such as burns caused by incorrect electrode placement.

Key terms

Return electrode (neutral electrode)
Large-area electrode attached to the patient in the monopolar technique so that the current leaves the body there at low density and without burns.
Monopolar / bipolar
In monopolar mode the current flows through the body to the return electrode; in bipolar mode only between the two tips of the instrument – gentler, but with less cutting power.
Coagulation
Clotting of tissue protein by heat; it seals blood vessels and stops bleeding.
