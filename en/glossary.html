Glossary

88 technical terms from Device Knowledge, briefly explained and linked to the devices where they appear.

A0 value
Measure of the inactivation of microorganisms by moist heat; an A0 value of 3000 corresponds, for example, to 90 °C for five minutes and is the usual value for the thermal disinfection of instruments.

Appears in: Washer-Disinfector (WD)

AED
Automated external defibrillator: analyses the heart rhythm independently and guides lay rescuers through the procedure.

Appears in: Defibrillator

Amalgam separator
Filter unit in the waste-water line that retains mercury-containing amalgam residues so they do not enter the sewer system.

Appears in: Dental treatment unit

Anti-scatter grid
A lead lamella grid in front of the detector that absorbs scattered radiation and thereby improves image contrast.

Appears in: Digital X-ray system

B-mode
Brightness mode – the standard two-dimensional greyscale image in which echo strength is shown as brightness.

Appears in: Ultrasound system (sonography)

Base excess
Calculated value indicating whether a disturbance of the acid-base balance is of metabolic origin.

Appears in: Blood gas analyser

Batch (load)
All items of one sterilization cycle; they are documented, checked and released together.

Appears in: Steam Sterilizer (Autoclave)

Batch documentation
Record of the process parameters of every sterilisation cycle, forming the basis for releasing the sterile goods.

Appears in: Dental autoclave (class B)

Bolus
A larger dose administered within a short time.

Appears in: Infusion pump / syringe pump

Bowie-Dick test
Daily test with an indicator pack that proves air removal and steam penetration in the chamber.

Appears in: Steam Sterilizer (Autoclave)

CAD/CAM
Computer-aided design and manufacturing of dental restorations based on digital data.

Appears in: Intraoral scanner

Calibration
Adjustment of the instrument using solutions of known concentration so that measured signals are correctly converted into concentrations.

Appears in: Clinical chemistry analyser

Capnography
Continuous measurement of the carbon dioxide content in exhaled air (etCO2) to monitor ventilation and circulation.

Appears in: Patient monitor, Anaesthesia machine

Cardiotocography (CTG)
Simultaneous recording of the fetal heart rate (cardio) and uterine contractions (toco).

Appears in: Fetal monitor (CTG)

Cardioversion
A synchronised shock timed to the heart's own activity, used to treat certain arrhythmias such as atrial fibrillation.

Appears in: Defibrillator

Cavitation
Formation and implosion of microscopic vapour bubbles in a liquid caused by ultrasound; the energy released loosens soil from surfaces.

Appears in: Ultrasonic Cleaner

CBCT
Cone beam computed tomography – a three-dimensional X-ray technique for the jaws and facial skeleton using a cone-shaped beam.

Appears in: Panoramic X-ray unit (OPG)

CE marking
Confirms that a medical device meets the applicable EU requirements. For higher risk classes, a Notified Body is involved.

Medical technology

Cephalometric radiograph (ceph)
Lateral skull radiograph used for orthodontic measurement of the skull and jaw relationship.

Appears in: Panoramic X-ray unit (OPG)

Circle system
Breathing system in which exhaled gas is returned to the patient after the carbon dioxide has been removed by soda lime – saving anaesthetic gas and preserving the moisture and heat of the breathing gas.

Appears in: Anaesthesia machine

Coagulation
Clotting of tissue protein by heat; it seals blood vessels and stops bleeding.

Appears in: Electrosurgical unit (ESU)

Cold light source
Powerful light source (today usually LED) whose light is guided into the endoscope through a fibre-optic cable; the infrared component is largely filtered out so that the tissue is hardly heated – the end of the light cable and the tip of the endoscope can nevertheless become hot.

Appears in: Endoscopy system (video endoscopy tower)

Colour rendering index (Ra/CRI)
Measure of how natural colours appear under a light source; high values are important for assessing tissue.

Appears in: Surgical light

Contra-angle handpiece
Attachment for the micromotor that transfers rotation to the bur; available with speed-increasing or speed-reducing gearing.

Appears in: Dental treatment unit

Degassing
Short idle run of the bath after filling so that dissolved air escapes; dissolved gases dampen cavitation and reduce cleaning performance.

Appears in: Ultrasonic Cleaner

Dialyser
The actual filter with a semi-permeable membrane in which substances are exchanged between blood and dialysis fluid.

Appears in: Dialysis machine

Differential count
Breakdown of white blood cells into their subgroups (e.g. neutrophils, lymphocytes, monocytes). A 3-part diff distinguishes three, a 5-part diff five cell populations.

Appears in: Haematology analyser

Doppler
A mode that measures the frequency shift of echoes from moving blood to display flow direction and speed, usually colour-coded.

Appears in: Ultrasound system (sonography)

Dose-area product (DAP)
A measure of the radiation delivered to the patient, calculated from dose and irradiated area; it must be documented for every exposure.

Appears in: Digital X-ray system

EDTA blood
Whole blood mixed with the anticoagulant EDTA so that the cells remain individually suspended for analysis.

Appears in: Haematology analyser

FiO2
Fraction of oxygen in the inhaled gas mixture (room air: 21 %).

Appears in: Ventilator

Fixed-angle rotor
Rotor with permanently inclined tube positions; allows higher speeds and shorter run times.

Appears in: Laboratory centrifuge

Flat-panel detector (DR)
An electronic sensor that converts X-rays directly into a digital image, replacing film and cassettes.

Appears in: Digital X-ray system

Flushing adapter
Connector that links hollow instruments such as cannulas or tubing to the water circuit so that internal surfaces are cleaned as well.

Appears in: Washer-Disinfector (WD)

Fractionated pre-vacuum
Repeated evacuation and steam flushing of the chamber before sterilisation to remove air completely.

Appears in: Dental autoclave (class B), Steam Sterilizer (Autoclave)

Gantry
The ring-shaped housing containing the rotating X-ray tube and detectors; the patient table moves through its opening.

Appears in: Computed tomography scanner (CT)

Haemoglobin
The red pigment in red blood cells that binds and transports oxygen.

Appears in: Pulse oximeter

Helix test
Process challenge device with a long, thin tube that simulates steam penetration into hollow items such as contra-angle handpieces.

Appears in: Dental autoclave (class B)

Hounsfield unit (HU)
The scale for attenuation values in CT images: water is 0 HU, air about −1000 HU, dense bone around +1000 HU and above.

Appears in: Computed tomography scanner (CT)

Impedance counting
Counting principle in which each cell briefly increases the electrical resistance as it passes through an aperture; the height of the pulse corresponds to the cell size.

Appears in: Haematology analyser

Insufflator
Device that delivers carbon dioxide into the abdominal cavity in a controlled manner to create space and visibility for the procedure.

Appears in: Endoscopy system (video endoscopy tower)

Ion-selective electrode (ISE)
Sensor whose electrical voltage depends on the concentration of a specific ion (e.g. potassium); the standard method for electrolytes.

Appears in: Clinical chemistry analyser

Laparoscopy
Minimally invasive surgery in the abdominal cavity through small incisions, through which the telescope and instruments are introduced (keyhole surgery).

Appears in: Endoscopy system (video endoscopy tower)

Lead
A view of the heart's electrical activity formed from the voltage difference between specific electrodes.

Appears in: ECG machine (12-lead)

MDR
Medical Device Regulation – EU Regulation 2017/745 on medical devices. It defines the requirements products must meet before being placed on the market.

Medical technology

Medical device logbook (Medizinproduktebuch)
Documentation for certain active medical devices: training, functional tests, maintenance and malfunctions.

Medical technology

Monopolar / bipolar
In monopolar mode the current flows through the body to the return electrode; in bipolar mode only between the two tips of the instrument – gentler, but with less cutting power.

Appears in: Electrosurgical unit (ESU)

MPDG & MPBetreibV
The German Medical Devices Law Implementation Act (MPDG) and the German Medical Devices Operator Ordinance (MPBetreibV) regulate, among other things, the obligations when operating and using medical devices.

Medical technology

NIBP
Non-invasive blood pressure measurement with an upper-arm cuff that the monitor inflates automatically at adjustable intervals.

Appears in: Patient monitor

Occlusion
A blockage of the infusion line, for example due to a kinked tube; the pump detects the rise in pressure and raises an alarm.

Appears in: Infusion pump / syringe pump

OPG / Orthopantomogram
Panoramic tomographic image of the entire jaw in which the tube and detector rotate around the head.

Appears in: Panoramic X-ray unit (OPG)

Partial pressure (pO2 / pCO2)
The share of the total pressure attributable to a dissolved gas; a measure of how much oxygen or carbon dioxide is dissolved in the blood.

Appears in: Blood gas analyser

PCA
Patient-controlled analgesia: patients trigger doses of pain medication themselves within predefined limits.

Appears in: Infusion pump / syringe pump

Peel test
Test in which the seal is pulled open by hand or in a test device; it must open evenly without fibre tear or gaps.

Appears in: Heat Sealer for Sterile Packaging

PEEP
Positive end-expiratory pressure: a residual pressure at the end of exhalation that keeps the alveoli open.

Appears in: Ventilator

Photometry
Measurement of the light absorption of a solution at a specific wavelength; it is the basic principle of most clinical chemistry tests.

Appears in: Clinical chemistry analyser

Plethysmogram
Waveform display of the pulsating light signal that makes signal quality and pulse visible.

Appears in: Pulse oximeter

POCT
Point-of-care testing: laboratory diagnostics performed directly at the patient, e.g. in the intensive care unit, without transporting the sample to the central laboratory.

Appears in: Blood gas analyser

Probe (transducer)
The handheld part that emits and receives sound waves. Shape and frequency determine penetration depth and resolution.

Appears in: Ultrasound system (sonography)

QRS complex
The characteristic spike in the ECG that represents the electrical excitation of the ventricles.

Appears in: ECG machine (12-lead)

Quench
The sudden loss of superconductivity, in which the liquid helium evaporates abruptly; the gas must be vented outside through a quench pipe.

Appears in: Magnetic resonance imaging scanner (MRI)

Relative centrifugal force (RCF)
Multiple of the acceleration due to gravity (g) acting on the sample; derived from speed and rotor radius and the decisive quantity for separation.

Appears in: Laboratory centrifuge

Resting ECG
A short standard recording taken lying down without physical exertion, usually over ten seconds.

Appears in: ECG machine (12-lead)

Return electrode (neutral electrode)
Large-area electrode attached to the patient in the monopolar technique so that the current leaves the body there at low density and without burns.

Appears in: Electrosurgical unit (ESU)

Satellite light
Second, usually smaller light head on the same ceiling system that supplements the main light and is positioned by the assistant.

Appears in: Surgical light

Scan body
Screw-on auxiliary component placed on an implant to make its position visible in the scan.

Appears in: Intraoral scanner

Seal (seam)
The bond between paper and film created by heat and pressure; it must be continuous, uniform and peelable so that the pack stays sterile and can be opened aseptically in the operating theatre.

Appears in: Heat Sealer for Sterile Packaging

Sequence (T1/T2)
A defined series of radiofrequency and gradient pulses. T1-weighted images show anatomy well, T2-weighted images highlight fluid and inflammation.

Appears in: Magnetic resonance imaging scanner (MRI)

Shadow management
Ability of a light to compensate for shadows cast by heads, hands or instruments by using many light sources from different angles.

Appears in: Surgical light

Shunt
A surgically created connection between an artery and a vein in the arm that provides a permanent vascular access with high blood flow.

Appears in: Dialysis machine

Spiral CT
Continuous table movement during tube rotation, so that a volume is acquired without gaps; the basis of most modern CT examinations – sequential (step-and-shoot) mode is still used, e.g. for ECG-triggered cardiac scans.

Appears in: Computed tomography scanner (CT)

SpO2
Oxygen saturation of haemoglobin in arterial blood measured by pulse oximetry, given as a percentage.

Appears in: Patient monitor

Spot check
A single short measurement for a quick check instead of continuous monitoring.

Appears in: Pulse oximeter

Sterile barrier system
The smallest packaging unit that prevents the entry of microorganisms and allows aseptic removal of the product.

Appears in: Heat Sealer for Sterile Packaging

STK / MTK
Safety-related and metrological inspections (German: sicherheitstechnische bzw. messtechnische Kontrollen) – regular checks that are mandatory for certain devices.

Medical technology

STL
Open file format for three-dimensional surface models that can be read by most CAD programs.

Appears in: Intraoral scanner

Swing-out rotor
Rotor whose sample buckets swing to a horizontal position during rotation; produces a flat separation layer and is well suited to blood samples.

Appears in: Laboratory centrifuge

Tesla (T)
Unit of magnetic flux density. Clinical MRI systems typically operate at 1.5 or 3 T – tens of thousands of times stronger than the Earth's magnetic field.

Appears in: Magnetic resonance imaging scanner (MRI)

Thermal disinfection
Disinfection solely by hot water without chemical disinfectants; it is reliable, easy to validate and leaves no residues.

Appears in: Washer-Disinfector (WD)

Tidal volume
The volume of gas delivered to the lungs with each breath.

Appears in: Ventilator

Tocodynamometer
Pressure transducer on the abdominal wall that registers contractions without measuring their actual strength.

Appears in: Fetal monitor (CTG)

Transducer
Piezoelectric component that converts the alternating voltage of the generator into mechanical vibrations of the tank.

Appears in: Ultrasonic Cleaner

Turbine
Air-driven handpiece with a very high rotational speed used to prepare hard tooth tissue.

Appears in: Dental treatment unit

UDI
Unique Device Identification – a unique product identifier for the traceability of medical devices.

Medical technology

Ultrafiltration
Removal of water from the blood by a pressure difference across the membrane.

Appears in: Dialysis machine

Vaporiser
Assembly that evaporates a liquid anaesthetic into the fresh gas flow in a controlled manner; each anaesthetic agent requires its own calibrated vaporiser.

Appears in: Anaesthesia machine

Variability
Natural beat-to-beat fluctuation of the fetal heart rate; an important sign of the baby's well-being.

Appears in: Fetal monitor (CTG)

Ventricular fibrillation
Uncoordinated electrical activity of the heart's ventricles in which the heart no longer pumps blood; a form of cardiac arrest.

Appears in: Defibrillator
